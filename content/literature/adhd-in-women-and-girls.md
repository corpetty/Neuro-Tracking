The [[dimensions/sex-and-gender|sex-and-gender dimensions note]] already makes the structural case: the male-skewed ADHD ratio largely reflects referral and ascertainment, not underlying prevalence (`hypothesis:sex-ratio-reflects-ascertainment`). This note develops what that under-recognition looks like clinically and why it matters — the inattentive presentation that hides it, the serious cost of missing it, and a female-specific hormonal axis that male-referenced models leave out. Every quotation below is verbatim from the cited source and is carried as an evidence triple in `interpretive/adhd-in-women-and-girls.jsonl`.

## 1. Why it is missed: a quieter presentation

The under-referral has a mechanism in the symptom profile. Hinshaw et al. (2022) summarise it: *"Girls and women with ADHD show a predominance of inattention and associated internalizing problems; boys and men display greater levels of hyperactive-impulsive symptoms and associated externalizing problems."* Inattention and internalizing are less disruptive to a classroom or a household than overt hyperactivity, so they are less likely to trigger a referral. The UK expert consensus (Young et al., 2020) reaches the same conclusion and names the cause: *"[t]here is evidence to suggest that the broad discrepancy in the ratio of males to females with diagnosed ADHD is due, at least in part, to lack of recognition and/or referral bias in females."* This is the same ascertainment argument, now grounded in a specific, quieter phenotype.

## 2. Why it matters: missing it is not benign

Under-identification would be a minor problem if the missed cases were mild. They are not. The Berkeley Girls with ADHD Longitudinal Study (Hinshaw et al., 2012) followed childhood-diagnosed girls for ten years and found that *"[p]articipants with childhood-diagnosed ADHD continued to display higher rates of ADHD and comorbid symptoms, showed more serious impairment (both global and specific), and had higher rates of suicide attempts and self-injury than the comparison sample, with effect sizes from medium to very large."* Hinshaw et al. (2022) restate the point at review level: females with ADHD show *"a particularly heightened risk for problems in close relationships and engagement in self-harm"* (`hypothesis:missed-adhd-in-females-harms-outcomes`). The quiet presentation is not a milder illness; it is a more easily overlooked one, with self-harm as a signature risk.

## 3. A female-specific axis: reproductive hormones

There is also a source of symptom variability that has no analogue in the male-referenced literature (`mechanism:reproductive-hormone-modulation`). Roberts et al. (2018), in a daily-diary study across the menstrual cycle, found that *"decreased levels of E2 in the context of increased levels of either P4 or T was associated with higher ADHD symptoms on the following day, particularly for those with high trait impulsivity"* — where E2 is estradiol, P4 progesterone, and T testosterone. Their conclusion is that *"ADHD symptoms may change across the menstrual cycle in response to endogenous steroid changes"* (`hypothesis:female-adhd-symptoms-hormone-sensitive`). Symptom severity, in other words, can move with the hormonal cycle while the underlying trait is unchanged — which a single-timepoint assessment or a male-referenced checklist will neither predict nor explain.

## Where this leaves the personal case

Two things carry over, independent of the reader's own sex.

- **The evidence base is sex-skewed, so borrow it carefully.** Much of what this notebook relies on is measured in samples with particular sex compositions — the Horváth trade-off study was 81% women, which the notebook already flags as `tension:sample-generalisability`. Sex differences in presentation and hormonal modulation are part of why a result in one sample may not transfer cleanly to a given person.
- **The late-identification theme is partly a sex story.** The [[dimensions/adult-late-diagnosis|adult and late-identified ADHD]] pattern — real ADHD carried quietly for years and recognised only later — is exactly the trajectory this literature describes for girls with an inattentive profile. And if the reader is a woman, the quieter presentation, the self-harm risk, and the hormonal modulation above apply directly, and are worth raising explicitly at a [[literature/adult-adhd-assessment|full evaluation]], where a single visit cannot capture cycle-dependent variability on its own.
