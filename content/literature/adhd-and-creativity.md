"ADHD is a creative superpower" is the sunniest of the popular claims, and, unlike the sugar myth, it has a genuine kernel. But the shape of the evidence is more specific and more interesting than the slogan — and getting it right matters, because an over-positive framing can be its own distortion. This note sets out where the creativity edge is real, where it is not, and what drives it. Every quotation below is verbatim from the cited source and is carried as an evidence triple in `interpretive/adhd-and-creativity.jsonl`.

## 1. Real, but trait-level and idea-generation-specific

Hoogman et al.'s (2020) review of 31 studies draws the key distinction the slogan misses: *"[m]ost studies find evidence for increased divergent thinking for those with high ADHD scores (subclinical) but not for those with the disorder (clinical)"* (`hypothesis:adhd-creativity-link-is-real-but-nuanced`). Divergent thinking — generating many novel ideas — is enhanced at the *trait* end, not necessarily in clinical ADHD. And the edge is narrow: *"[w]e found no evidence for increased convergent thinking abilities in ADHD, nor did we find an overall negative effect of psychostimulants on creativity."* So it is an idea-*generation* advantage, not idea-*refinement*, and — reassuringly — treating ADHD does not appear to cost creativity. This is, notably, a dimensional finding: the creative feature tracks the trait continuum ([[literature/dimensional-continuum-adhd|dimensional models]]), not the impairment.

## 2. What drives the real-world achievement: motivation and fit

Beyond lab tasks, real-world creative achievement is genuinely elevated: Hoogman et al. found *"[t]he rates of creative abilities/achievements were high among both clinical and subclinical groups,"* and Boot et al. (2020) replicated it — *"[a]dults with ADHD reported more real-world creative achievements"* (`hypothesis:adhd-creative-achievement-is-motivation-and-domain-driven`). Crucially, they identified *why*: *"goal-directed motivation may drive the enhanced real-world creative achievements of people with ADHD,"* with people selectively excelling in domains that fit their skills and interests. That is the [[literature/adhd-and-motivation|reward-motivation account]] (`mechanism:reward-motivation`) pointed at a strength: creativity flourishes exactly where interest and reward align — the same contingency that makes dull tasks so hard makes chosen ones a place to shine.

## Where this leaves the personal case

- **Take the strength, drop the slogan.** §1 says a creative edge is plausible — at the trait level, for idea generation, in interest-aligned domains — but the "superpower" framing overstates it and can quietly minimise the real difficulties this whole notebook documents. It is an *edge*, not a compensation that cancels the impairment.
- **It is not diagnostic, in either direction.** Being creative does not confirm ADHD, and not feeling especially creative does not rule it out; the creativity link is a correlate of the trait, not a test.
- **Treatment need not cost it.** A common worry — that medication will flatten creativity — is not borne out (§1), which removes one reason people hesitate over an [[literature/adult-adhd-assessment|evaluation]] or treatment.
