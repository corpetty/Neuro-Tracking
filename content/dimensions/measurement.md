Once you accept that "the ADHD dimension" lives at [four different levels](orthogonality.md), the measurement question splits: *which level are you trying to gauge, and with what?* Each level has its own instrument family, and the families do not agree with each other. This note maps them, then draws the consequence for a real assessment.

## Ratings and tasks are measuring different things

The most important — and least intuitive — fact about ADHD measurement is that rating scales and performance tasks aimed at the "same" construct barely correlate. In a review of 20 studies, Toplak, West & Stanovich (2013) found that *"Only 68 (24%) of the 286 relevant correlations reported in these studies were statistically significant, and the overall median correlation was only .19."* Their conclusion is that *"performance-based and rating measures of executive function assess different underlying mental constructs"* — ratings capture success in everyday goal pursuit, tasks capture processing efficiency in a controlled setting. Neither is wrong; they are different levels of cognition. So a high ASRS score (a rating) and a poor stop-signal result (a task) are not two looks at one thing — they are two things (`hypothesis:rating-task-dissociation`).

## The mechanism axes and how they are gauged

At the mechanism level, each dissociable process has a characteristic task:

- **Response inhibition** — stop-signal task, go/no-go (`instrument:stop-signal-task`; also the [[literature/ADHD-traits-inhibition-learning|Cognitive Trade-off Task]]).
- **Sustained attention & reaction-time variability** — the continuous performance test (`instrument:continuous-performance-test`). Reaction-time variability is one of the most robust ADHD findings: Kofler et al.'s (2013) meta-analysis of 319 studies reported that *"children/adolescents (Hedges' g = 0.76) and adults (g = 0.46) with ADHD demonstrated greater RT variability relative to TD groups."*
- **Working memory** — n-back and complex-span tasks (`instrument:working-memory-task`).
- **Reward / delay aversion** — delay-discounting and choice-delay paradigms (`instrument:delay-discounting-task`); ADHD is associated with steeper discounting of delayed rewards (Jackson & MacKillop, 2016).
- **Temporal processing** and **arousal / state regulation** round out the set.

## The pathways are dissociable — and no deficit is universal

Why measure several mechanisms rather than one? Because ADHD is neurocognitively heterogeneous. Sonuga-Barke, Bitsakou & Thompson (2010) found that *"For ADHD patients, the co-occurrence of inhibitory, temporal processing and delay-related deficits was no greater than expected by chance with substantial groups of patients showing only one problem"* — the routes are independent, and a person can travel any one of them (`hypothesis:multiple-neurocognitive-pathways`). Nigg et al. (2005) put the corollary bluntly: research should reckon with *"the probability that only a subset of behaviorally defined children will have a deficit in a given neurocognitive mechanism believed to contribute to the disorder."* Coghill et al. (2014) quantified it — for any single cognitive domain, only roughly a fifth to a third of children with ADHD are actually impaired. Executive-function weakness, in Willcutt et al.'s (2005) meta-analytic phrase, is *neither necessary nor sufficient*.

## The rating families

At the phenotype level the instruments are all dimensional, differing mainly in how they score the continuum: the [[about-me/diagnostics|ASRS v1.1]] (a summed screener), the Conners CAARS and Barkley BAARS-IV (norm-referenced T-scores against a population sample), and the SWAN (a symmetric strengths-to-weaknesses scale that yields roughly normal population scores). At the nosology level, maladaptive-trait inventories like the PID-5 place a person on the disinhibited-externalizing spectrum rather than in the ADHD box.

## No single measure is diagnostic

The measurement facts converge on one conclusion, and it is a caution as much as a method. Continuous performance tests, the best-studied objective measure, do not settle the question: a systematic review found *"mixed findings with regard to the use of CPTs to assess and manage medication, with contrasting evidence on their ability to support clinical decision-making"* (Hall et al., 2016), and an earlier review concluded the leading tasks need *"further efforts to evaluate the validity of these measures … before they can be used for more than experimental purposes"* (Nichols & Waschbusch, 2004). Reaction-time variability, robust as it is, is not ADHD-specific — Kofler et al. found *"RT variability is not specific to ADHD,"* with adults indistinguishable from other clinical groups. And rating scales *"should not be relied upon solely to clinically diagnose adult ADHD"* (behavioural-rating-scale review, 2023). The organising verdict, in the plainest terms available: *"There is no single test to diagnose ADHD. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation is necessary to establish a diagnosis, rule out other causes, and determine the presence or absence of co-existing conditions"* (CHADD National Resource Center). This is `hypothesis:no-single-measure-is-diagnostic`.

## What this means for the next step

For the personal case the implication is direct (`question:next-clinical-step`). The one data point on record is a rating (ASRS Part A). Ratings and tasks measure different things; tasks are dissociable and none is diagnostic on its own; and the mimics — anxiety, sleep, mood — are separable from ADHD only at the mechanism level. So the productive next step is not another questionnaire but a multi-method evaluation that samples across levels, with the explicit goal of tracing the internal restlessness to its source rather than confirming a label — exactly what `tension:adhd-vs-anxiety-internal-restlessness` demands.
